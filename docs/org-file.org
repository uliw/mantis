* Carbonate Chemistry Methods

** Corrections for temperature, salinity and pressure

The aqueous inorganic carbon system in seawater is parameterized by the stoichiometric dissociation constants of carbonic acid ($K_1^*, K_2^*$) and the stoichiometric solubility product of calcite ($K'_{sp}$). In MANTIS, these equilibrium constants are computed dynamically as continuous functions of local in-situ temperature ($T$), practical salinity ($S$), and hydrostatic pressure ($P$).

*** Carbonic Acid Dissociation Constants ($K_1^*, K_2^*$)

At atmospheric pressure ($P = 1\ \text{bar}$), the apparent first and second dissociation constants of carbonic acid are calculated using the formulations of Lueker, Dickson, and Keeling (2000), which refit the spectrophotometric measurements of Mehrbach et al. (1973) onto the Total pH scale:

$$\log_{10} K_1^0 = -\frac{3633.86}{T} + 61.2172 - 9.6777 \ln T - 0.011555 S + 0.0001152 S^2$$

$$\log_{10} K_2^0 = -\frac{471.78}{T} - 25.9290 + 3.16967 \ln T - 0.01781 S + 0.0001122 S^2$$

where $T$ is absolute temperature in Kelvin ($T = T_C + 273.15$, with $T_C$ in $^\circ\text{C}$), and $S$ is salinity on the practical salinity scale (valid for $19 \le S \le 43$ and $2 \le T_C \le 35\ ^\circ\text{C}$).

Hydrostatic pressure corrections for $K_1^*$ and $K_2^*$ under depth ($P > 1\ \text{bar}$) follow Millero (1995):

$$\ln \left( \frac{K_i(P)}{K_i^0} \right) = \frac{-\Delta V_i + 0.5 \kappa_i P}{R T} P \quad (i = 1, 2)$$

where $R = 83.1446\ \text{cm}^3 \cdot \text{bar} \cdot \text{mol}^{-1} \cdot \text{K}^{-1}$, $P$ is pressure in bar, and the partial molal volume change ($\Delta V_i$, $\text{cm}^3 \cdot \text{mol}^{-1}$) and molal compressibility change ($\kappa_i$, $\text{cm}^3 \cdot \text{mol}^{-1} \cdot \text{bar}^{-1}$) are given by:

$$\Delta V_1 = -25.5 + 0.1271 T_C, \quad \kappa_1 = (-3.08 + 0.0877 T_C) \times 10^{-3}$$

$$\Delta V_2 = -15.82 - 0.0219 T_C, \quad \kappa_2 = (1.13 - 0.1475 T_C) \times 10^{-3}$$

*** Calcite Solubility Product ($K'_{sp}$)

The stoichiometric solubility product of calcite at 1 atm total pressure is calculated using the empirical formulation of Mucci (1983):

$$\log_{10} K_{sp}^0 = -171.9065 - 0.077993 T + \frac{2839.319}{T} + 71.595 \log_{10} T + \left(-0.77712 + 0.0028426 T + \frac{178.34}{T}\right) \sqrt{S} - 0.07711 S + 0.0041249 S^{1.5}$$

where $K_{sp}^0$ is defined on the molal concentration scale ($(\text{mol} \cdot \text{kg}_{\text{sw}}^{-1})^2$). Hydrostatic pressure effects on calcite solubility are incorporated using the partial molal volume and compressibility formulation of Ingle (1975) and Millero (1979, 1995):

$$\ln \left( \frac{K'_{sp}(P)}{K_{sp}^0} \right) = \frac{-\Delta V_{Ca} + 0.5 \kappa_{Ca} P}{R T} P$$

with:

$$\Delta V_{Ca} = -48.76 + 0.5304 T_C, \quad \kappa_{Ca} = (-11.76 + 0.3692 T_C) \times 10^{-3}$$

*** Unit Conversions and Seawater Density

In MANTIS, reaction-transport equations for dissolved solutes are formulated in volumetric concentration units ($\text{mol} \cdot \text{m}^{-3} = \text{mmol} \cdot \text{L}^{-1}$). To convert stoichiometric constants between the gravimetric scale ($\text{mol} \cdot \text{kg}_{\text{sw}}^{-1}$) and volumetric scale ($\text{mol} \cdot \text{m}^{-3}$), in-situ seawater density $\rho_{\text{sw}}(T_C, S)$ ($\text{kg} \cdot \text{m}^{-3}$) is calculated using the UNESCO 1981 / Millero & Poisson (1981) international equation of state:

$$K'_{sp}\ (\text{mol}^2 \cdot \text{m}^{-6}) = K'_{sp}\ (\text{mol}^2 \cdot \text{kg}_{\text{sw}}^{-2}) \times \rho_{\text{sw}}^2$$

Because $[H^+] = 10^{-\text{pH}}$ is expressed on the standard Total pH scale, the dissociation constants $K_1^*$ and $K_2^*$ are preserved on the Total activity scale ($pK_1 \approx 6.00, pK_2 \approx 9.22$), ensuring that speciation fractions ($\alpha_1, \alpha_2$) remain dimensionless.

** Choosing a calcite precipitation rate

In marine porewater diagenesis, the precipitation of calcium carbonate ($\text{CaCO}_3$) cannot be represented accurately by pure abiotic rate constants derived from dilute laboratory solutions. In seawater, hydrated magnesium ions ($\text{Mg}^{2+}$), dissolved organic matter, and sulfate ions ($\text{SO}_4^{2-}$) adsorb strongly onto mineral growth steps, retarding calcite nucleation and surface growth by several orders of magnitude (Zhong & Mucci, 1989; Morse et al., 2007).

Using abiotic crystal growth constants ($k_{\text{precip}} \sim 10^{-4}\ \text{mol} \cdot \text{m}^{-3} \cdot \text{s}^{-1} \approx 3155\ \text{mol} \cdot \text{m}^{-3} \cdot \text{yr}^{-1}$) causes extreme overprediction of authigenic carbonate (e.g. accumulating upwards of $50\ \text{wt}\%$ $\text{CaCO}_3$ in the sediment within decades) and imposes severe numerical timestep stiffness.

Following standard diagenetic modeling literature (Wang & Van Cappellen, 1996; Jourabchi et al., 2005), the kinetic rate of calcite precipitation is parameterized as a function of the saturation state ($\Omega = [\text{Ca}^{2+}][\text{CO}_3^{2-}] / K'_{sp}$):

$$R_{\text{precip}} = k_{\text{precip}} \cdot \max(\Omega - 1, 0) \cdot S(\Omega - 1)$$

$$R_{\text{diss}} = k_{\text{diss}} \cdot \max(1 - \Omega, 0) \cdot S(1 - \Omega) \cdot [\text{CaCO}_3]$$

where $S(\cdot)$ represents a cubic smooth transition function avoiding derivative discontinuities near equilibrium.

The calibrated kinetic constants are set according to Jourabchi, Van Cappellen, and Regnier (2005) and Wang and Van Cappellen (1996):
- **Precipitation rate constant**: $k_{\text{precip}} = 1.0\ \text{mol} \cdot \text{m}^{-3} \cdot \text{yr}^{-1} \approx 3.17 \times 10^{-8}\ \text{mol} \cdot \text{m}^{-3} \cdot \text{s}^{-1}$ (Jourabchi et al., 2005, Table 2).
- **Dissolution rate constant**: $k_{\text{diss}} = 3.65\ \text{yr}^{-1} = 0.01\ \text{day}^{-1} \approx 1.16 \times 10^{-7}\ \text{s}^{-1}$ (normalized to local solid $[\text{CaCO}_3]$; Jourabchi et al., 2005; Wang & Van Cappellen, 1996).

These rate constants provide realistic, field-validated authigenic accumulation rates that preserve natural sediment mineralogy while permitting multi-year numerical timesteps.

** Using the mantis carbonate chemistry module

To maintain strict modularity and prevent interference with non-carbonate models, carbonate chemistry in MANTIS is fully gated. Unless explicitly configured, variables such as $\text{TA}$, $\text{DIC}$, $\text{H}^+$, $\text{Ca}^{2+}$, and $\text{CaCO}_3$ are treated as standard independent transport species without thermodynamic speciation or vector prefilling.

*** Including carbonate equilibrium calculations

To activate marine carbonate equilibrium speciation, pass the dictionary =carbonate_equilibrium_chemistry= to =model.configure(...)=:

#+BEGIN_SRC python
model.configure(
    temp=[10.0, 10.1],      # in °C (used for in-situ T)
    carbonate_equilibrium_chemistry={
        "pressure": 1.0,     # in bar (optional, default: 1.0)
        "salinity": 34.0,    # in PSU (optional, default: 34.0)
        "TA": 2.4,           # in mol/m^3 = mmol/L
        "DIC": 2.2,          # in mol/m^3 = mmol/L
        "adjust_omega": True # in equilibrium with calcite (default: True)
    }
)
#+END_SRC

When enabled, MANTIS:
1. Calculates $K_1^*, K_2^*, K'_{sp}$, and $\rho_{\text{sw}}$ at the specified temperature, salinity, and pressure.
2. Checks that dissolved calcium (=Ca2=) is defined with a valid positive boundary concentration. If =Ca2= is missing, MANTIS halts with an error.
3. Resolves the boundary conditions according to the chosen degrees of freedom.
4. Updates the model parameter namespace (=mp.is_carbonate = True=, =mp.pK1_carb=, =mp.pK2_carb=, =mp.Ksp_calcite=).
5. Registers vectorized carbonate speciation routines in the PDE solver, live plotting, and post-processing diagnostics.

*** Choosing boundary conditions

Because the marine inorganic carbon system has *exactly two thermodynamic degrees of freedom* at fixed $(T, S, P)$, boundary conditions must respect thermodynamic constraints:

- **Rule 1: Maximum of two controls.** Specify at most two of $\{\text{TA}, \text{DIC}, \text{pH}\}$ in =carbonate_equilibrium_chemistry=. Specifying all three produces a fatal overdefinition error.
- **Rule 2: Complete override of species.py.** Whenever =carbonate_equilibrium_chemistry= is specified, any boundary conditions defined for carbonate species (=TA=, =DIC=, =Hplus=) in =species.py= are disregarded, and all boundary choices and porewater vector initializations are determined solely by =carbonate_equilibrium_chemistry=.
- **Rule 3: Calcium requirement.** The species =Ca2= must be present in the model species definitions with a defined concentration at $z = 0$.

**** Equilibrium Mode (=adjust_omega = True=)

Setting =adjust_omega = True= enforces thermodynamic equilibrium with respect to calcite ($\Omega = 1.00$) at the sediment-water interface ($z = 0$). Because $\Omega = 1.00$ fixes $[\text{CO}_3^{2-}]_{\text{eq}} = K'_{sp} / [\text{Ca}^{2+}]_{\text{top}}$, the system is reduced to one free parameter.

MANTIS supports all three boundary combinations:
1. **$\text{pH}$ & $\text{DIC}$ specified $\rightarrow$ adjust $\text{TA}$**:
   From $\text{DIC}$ and $[\text{CO}_3^{2-}]_{\text{eq}}$, the equilibrium hydrogen ion concentration $[H^+]_{\text{eq}}$ and $\text{TA}_{\text{eq}} = [\text{HCO}_3^-] + 2[\text{CO}_3^{2-}]_{\text{eq}}$ are uniquely determined. $\text{TA}$ is adjusted to $\text{TA}_{\text{eq}}$.
2. **$\text{pH}$ & $\text{TA}$ specified $\rightarrow$ adjust $\text{DIC}$**:
   From $\text{TA}$ and $[\text{CO}_3^{2-}]_{\text{eq}}$, $[\text{HCO}_3^-] = \text{TA} - 2[\text{CO}_3^{2-}]_{\text{eq}}$, $[H^+]_{\text{eq}} = K_2^* [\text{HCO}_3^-] / [\text{CO}_3^{2-}]_{\text{eq}}$, and $\text{DIC}_{\text{eq}}$ are uniquely calculated. $\text{DIC}$ is adjusted to $\text{DIC}_{\text{eq}}$.
3. **$\text{DIC}$ & $\text{TA}$ specified $\rightarrow$ adjust $\text{pH}$**:
   From $\text{DIC}$ and $[\text{CO}_3^{2-}]_{\text{eq}}$, $[H^+]_{\text{eq}}$ is calculated, and $\text{TA}$ is aligned to $\text{TA}_{\text{eq}}$, ensuring exact self-consistency.

In addition, MANTIS initializes the porewater concentration vectors of the sediment column to this equilibrium state, avoiding artificial boundary shock and allowing the adaptive timestep controller to accelerate smoothly to multi-year steps.

**** Non-Equilibrium Mode (=adjust_omega = False=)

Setting =adjust_omega = False= preserves boundary conditions as specified:
- Exactly two of $\{\text{TA}, \text{DIC}, \text{pH}\}$ must be provided.
- MANTIS solves the third variable using exact quadratic carbonate speciation.
- The in-situ saturation state $\Omega = [\text{Ca}^{2+}][\text{CO}_3^{2-}] / K'_{sp}$ is calculated and reported in the console notice (indicating supersaturation or undersaturation).
